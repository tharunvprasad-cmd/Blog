BOOK REVIEW BLOG – REPORT

Name: Tharun v prasad Class: CSE AI sem3 Institution: Adi Shankara Institute of Engineering and Technology Activity: Blog featuring reviews of five recently read books

Introduction

Reading books helps us improve our knowledge, imagination, vocabulary and understanding of different people and situations. As part of this activity, I created a blog containing reviews of five books. The reviews include the basic details of each book, a short description of its story or ideas, important themes, and my personal opinion.

1. The Alchemist – Paulo Coelho

Genre: Fiction / Inspirational

Review: The Alchemist follows Santiago, a young shepherd who dreams of finding a treasure near the Egyptian pyramids. During his journey, he meets different people who teach him about dreams, courage, love and the importance of following his own path. The book presents the idea that a person should not give up on a meaningful dream simply because the journey is difficult. I liked the simple writing style and the motivational message of the story. It is an easy book to read but gives the reader several ideas to think about.

2. Atomic Habits – James Clear

Genre: Self-help / Personal Development

Review: Atomic Habits explains how small habits can produce significant changes over time. James Clear discusses how habits are formed and gives practical ideas for building good habits and reducing bad ones. The book focuses on making small improvements instead of depending only on motivation. I found the examples practical and easy to understand. The main lesson I took from the book is that consistent small actions can gradually create major changes in our lives.

3. The Kite Runner – Khaled Hosseini

Genre: Historical Fiction / Drama

Review: The Kite Runner tells the story of Amir and Hassan and explores their friendship, family relationships, guilt and redemption. The story is set partly in Afghanistan and shows how political and social changes affect ordinary people. Amir carries guilt from an event in his childhood and later tries to make amends for his past actions. The novel made me think about friendship, responsibility and the consequences of our choices. Its emotional storytelling and strong characters make it memorable.

4. 1984 – George Orwell

Genre: Dystopian Fiction / Political Fiction

Review: 1984 presents a dystopian society where the government exercises extreme control over information, language and people's lives. The main character, Winston Smith, begins to question the system around him. The novel explores surveillance, propaganda, censorship, truth and individual freedom. Although it was written decades ago, its ideas about information and control remain interesting to study. I found the atmosphere of the novel serious and thought-provoking.

Common Themes from the First Four Books

These books are very different in genre, but they all contain ideas about human choices and personal growth. The Alchemist focuses on dreams and determination, Atomic Habits focuses on everyday improvement, The Kite Runner focuses on friendship and responsibility, and 1984 focuses on freedom, truth and control. Reading different genres helped me understand that books can provide entertainment while also making us think about real life.

5. The Silent Patient – Alex Michaelides

Genre: Psychological Thriller / Mystery

Review: The Silent Patient is a mystery thriller centred on Alicia Berenson, a painter who becomes silent after a shocking incident involving her husband. A psychotherapist named Theo Faber becomes interested in her case and tries to understand what happened. The story gradually reveals information through different characters and timelines. I enjoyed the mystery and the way the author keeps the reader interested in finding the truth. The ending changes how earlier events can be understood, which makes the book engaging.

Conclusion

Creating the blog helped me practise reading, summarising, writing and presenting information in a simple digital format. The five books also exposed me to different genres, including fiction, self-help, historical fiction, dystopian fiction and psychological thriller. Each book gave me a different idea or perspective. Overall, this activity helped me understand that regular reading can improve both knowledge and communication skills.
